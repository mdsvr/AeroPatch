# Train the 4B on its own verified fixes

The fine-tune lost because **the 4B wrote none of the edits in dataset v2**. Its 541 targets are 275 upstream advisory commits, 108 bug-fix patches, 105 synthetic fixes written by the 9B teacher and 53 repair turns whose answer is the scenario's reference fix, and only the one-line RATIONALE is in the 4B's own words ([stats-v2](../training/stats-v2.md)). Supervised training on targets a strong post-trained model would not have written pulls it away from its base on the very task it already does well. In AeroPatch that shows up mostly as worse first edits (**28 first-attempt resolves on test for the untuned control against 24 and 22** for the two trainings) and as more edits that break existing behaviour. STUCK is the visible sign of a narrowed sampling distribution rather than the main source of lost points ([NOTES.md](../NOTES.md)). The fixes, cheapest first, run from measurement through a free adapter diagnostic to three training rungs, all summarised in the rung table below. First measure properly and set the inference-side bar; then sweep the existing adapter's scale as a diagnostic; then measure the base model's headroom on the 105 leakage-clean synthetic scenarios; then retrain from base only on the 4B's own sandbox-passing outputs at a 4-10x lower learning rate; then add preference pairs that reject REGRESSION; and only then attempt multi-turn GRPO with a pytest reward on Kaggle. Expect the on-policy retrain to restore parity (**about 36-41 of 54, STUCK near zero**), not to beat the base. Published gains from the stronger rungs at 4-8B are **+4 to +6 points, about 2-3 of 54 runs**, below the project's own 4-run rule for a gain. Proving any real improvement therefore needs more runs, a bigger evaluation pool and a same-day control, because three untrained variants (Q5_K_M, Q8_0, thinking on) already score **41 against the control's 38**.

## Every edit in v2 came from someone other than the 4B

The strongest 2025-2026 account of a fine-tune falling below its base is distribution shift from off-policy targets. RL's Razor found that forgetting tracks the KL divergence between tuned and base model, measured on the new task's own inputs (**R² = 0.71** in its LLM experiments), and that "SFT can converge to distributions arbitrarily far from the base model" ([RL's Razor](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04259)). Retaining by Doing measured the same split on Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct: SFT on Countdown gained **10.4 points while losing 29.2 on other tasks**, and GRPO on the same target gained 29.2 and lost nothing (+0.3). The authors credit on-policy data, not the KL penalty ([Retaining by Doing](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18874v3)). Most forgetting numbers concern other tasks, but there is an on-target analogue: Olmo-3-7B-Think trained on answer-only targets **fell from 31.2 to 23.5 on the task it was trained for**, and on-policy self-distillation lifted it to 43.7 instead ([SDFT 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19897)). Human-written targets make the shift worse because they carry more tokens the student finds surprising; Mistral-7B tuned on MBPP scored **0.276 with human targets against 0.354 with model-written ones** ([I Learn Better](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11192)). Qwen3's small models were built by strong-to-weak distillation ending in an on-policy phase ([Qwen3 report](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09388v1)), which places the family on a teacher-shaped mode that off-policy SFT disturbs easily. No Qwen3.5 report was found to confirm the same for this model.

The project's own runs locate the loss more precisely than the literature can.

| Test, 2 runs each | Untuned control | v2, first training | v2, retrained |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resolved, of 54 | 38 | 32 | 29 |
| Resolved at attempt 1 | 28 | 24 | 22 |
| Failed first attempts later repaired | 10 of 26 (38%) | 8 of 30 (27%) | 7 of 32 (22%) |
| Runs ending `STUCK` | 0 | 9 | 11 |
| Real CVEs (Tier B), of 20 | 7 | 3 | 4 |

Counts come from ([NOTES.md](../NOTES.md)); the repaired shares are my arithmetic from them. **About two-thirds of the gap opens at the first attempt**, and the repair step recovers a smaller share afterwards. The project's data does not support blaming the 53 offline repair turns for the lost points. On dev in v1, failed first attempts were repaired about as often after tuning as before (3 of 16 for the control, 5 of 24 and 4 of 23 for the checkpoints), and STUCK mostly relabelled runs that were failing anyway ([NOTES.md](../NOTES.md)). The harness also retries an identical edit once at temperature 0.8 before it ends a run as STUCK ([NOTES.md](../NOTES.md)), so **a STUCK run means the tuned model produced the same edit even at 0.8**, which points to collapsed sampling diversity. The literature describes that collapse: SFT on offline correction pairs left SCoRe's model "overly conservative", changing **0% of answers** on retry ([SCoRe](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12917)), and Llama 3.1 Instruct "frequently output the same code solution despite inline feedback" ([RLEF](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02089)).

REGRESSION has a data-side explanation. Commit-mined vulnerability fixes are noisy. Raw Python function-level labels were **40.4% accurate** before cleaning, mostly because commits bundle unrelated refactorings ([PyVul, MSR 2026](https://2026.msrconf.org/details/msr-2026-technical-papers/24/An-Empirical-Study-of-Vulnerabilities-in-Python-Packages-and-Their-Detection)). In one cleaned C/C++ repair dataset, 56% of sampled labels were wrong ([R+R](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.06387v1)), and **16.7% of CVEs** carry patches that cannot be judged from the function alone ([Mono](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03651)). Fitting fixes the model cannot infer from its context is the "Unknown example" case, which raises hallucination linearly once learned ([Gekhman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05904)); in code its analogue is a confident edit that changes behaviour the regression suite checks. Offline secure-code training shows the same price elsewhere: SafeCoder and ProSec **cut Qwen2.5-Coder-7B's HumanEval+ from 79.88 to 62.07 and 59.51** ([SecCoderX](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07422v1)).

The training settings gave no safety margin. LoRA on a post-trained Qwen3-8B "still forgets its original post-training behaviors" ([Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)), and a fixed alpha equal to r plus a high learning rate both increase LoRA forgetting ([Illusion of Equivalence](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21228v3)). On Qwen3-4B itself, full fine-tuning at **lr 1e-6 kept general ability at 0.784 against 0.543 at 5e-6**, for a domain score of 0.548 against 0.578 ([SFT Doesn't Always Hurt](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20758)). With LoRA's optimal rate at about 10x full fine-tuning ([LoRA Without Regret](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/)), that points to roughly **1e-5 to 5e-5 where AeroPatch used 2e-4**, an extrapolation because the 10x rule was measured on loss, not forgetting. The damage arrives early: v1's checkpoints at steps 32, 64 and 128 all scored 20 of 39 on dev against the control's 26 ([NOTES.md](../NOTES.md)), so fewer steps will not fix it.

It follows that the "larger dataset" option in the project's Next list is the wrong lever as specified. A second 26-hour generation run, more bug fixes and the 258 merge-commit advisories would all add reference-fix targets. Better models gain less from more supervised data ([RFT scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01825)), self-generated data beats a teacher's when the teacher is not clearly stronger ([SelfCodeAlign](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.24198)), and the 9B teacher is not clearly stronger than this 4B. On Tier C, the kind of data v2 was trained on, both models resolve 9 of 10: the base already does what the training taught. Two confounds remain untested, a chat-template difference between Unsloth training and Ollama serving in how the empty think block is handled, and Q4_K_M quantisation of the merged delta.

## Thinking mode alone scores 41, so the bar is higher than 38

At 54 runs and a resolve rate near 0.7, one binomial standard error is about **±3.4 runs**, and paired scenarios make the effective error larger; the first v2's own two test runs scored 18 and 14 ([NOTES.md](../NOTES.md)). The project's rule (a gain is 4 or more of 54 above the control and not below it on Tier B) is right to ignore 1-3 run differences. The control's 38 also sits low: **Q5_K_M, Q8_0 and thinking-on each scored 41** on the same split, each judged "no difference shown", and the retrain's p = 0.022 was computed against control runs five days older and not alternated with it ([NOTES.md](../NOTES.md)). Every candidate below should be judged against alternated same-day control runs, at **four runs per model rather than two**.

Test has now judged two fine-tunes, and the 13-scenario dev set cannot resolve a 2-3 run change, so choices between rungs belong on a larger dev pool, with test kept for one final verdict. The best free candidates are executable Python repair sets from before the 2026 cutoff: PatchEval, with **230 sandboxed CVEs** across Go, JS and Python under an Apache-2.0 repo ([PatchEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11019v1)), and Vul4Py, with **100 Python vulnerabilities** carrying both exploit and functional tests but no licence file, so internal use only ([Vul4Py](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00692v1)). Both are mined from the same advisory streams as the project's data, so they need the overlap and dedupe checks first, and splitting them by project into a dev half and a training-environment half serves both needs. Configuration must match too, including thinking mode. If thinking-on becomes the served configuration, any fine-tune must be judged with thinking on, and new targets must be sampled with thinking on. Otherwise v3 repeats the answer-only-SFT-on-a-thinking-model setup that dropped Olmo-3 below its base ([SDFT 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19897)).

Training also has to beat what sampling achieves at the same sandbox budget, and the project already holds those numbers. On test the three-attempt loop resolves **70% against 61% for pass@3** and 67% for pass@5 at temperature 0.7, while on the 10 real CVEs it is reversed, **45% for pass@3 against 35% for the loop** ([NOTES.md](../NOTES.md)). The literature predicts this. Self-repair gains are "often modest" once cost is counted, and a fixed budget is better spent on diverse first attempts than on more repairs ([Olausson et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09896)); detailed feedback reduces sample diversity, and untrained multi-turn adds "usually less than +2%" ([Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08105)); an 8B instruct model with best-of-5 scored 69.7 on MBPP against 62.1 for muCode's trained single-sample model ([muCode](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20380)). Three zero-GPU configurations therefore deserve measurement before any training, since they lift the base and the tuned model alike: two or three diverse first attempts each repaired once, a fresh sample without feedback in place of ending at STUCK, and feedback cut to failing test names and assertion lines. The last two are my inferences; no paper measures them on repair.

Each rung should report totals, first-attempt resolves, the repaired share of failed first attempts, STUCK and REGRESSION counts, Tier B separately, and the paired McNemar test and bootstrap interval that `aeropatch report` already prints. One new metric belongs beside them: a **distinct-edit rate**, from k=8 samples at temperature 0.7 on dev for the base and each candidate, counting distinct edits after rebasing. It measures the collapse behind STUCK directly and costs about 10-20 laptop minutes per model ([feasibility note](../research_notes/AeroPatch%20model%20improvement/feasibility_t4_laptop.md)).

## Five rungs, from a free adapter sweep to Kaggle GRPO

| Rung | Cost | Measure before → after | Expected result | Go on if |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Measurement and inference baseline | 0 GPU-h; about a day of laptop runs | Same-day control (4 runs), hybrid sampling config, distinct-edit rate | Control 38-41; hybrid at or above the loop on Tier B | Always first |
| 1. Alpha sweep of the v2 adapter (s = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75) | About 1-1.5 Kaggle GPU-h, or 0 with `llama-server --lora-scaled` | Dev-pool total, first-attempt resolves, STUCK and distinct edits per scale | A monotone curve from v2 back to base; no scale above base | Diagnostic only |
| 2. On-policy rollouts on the leakage-clean scenarios | About 3-4 laptop-h | pass@1, pass@8, partly-solved count, loop rollouts with repairs | pass@8 well above pass@1 (the student pass went 59% → 83% within three attempts) | pass@8 − pass@1 of about 10 points or more (my threshold) |
| 3. On-policy rejection-sampling fine-tune (RFT) from base | About 1-2 GPU-h (my estimate) | Rung 0 metrics, plus mean NLL of targets under the base | Test 36-41; STUCK 0-2; Tier B 6-8 only if synthetic gains transfer | Parity reached |
| 4. SimPO/IPO on own pass/fail pairs | About 0.5 h smoke plus 1.5-3 GPU-h | REGRESSION count, repaired share, first-attempt resolves | +0 to +3 runs over RFT, fewer REGRESSION | Smoke passes; at least about 100 pairs |
| 5. Multi-turn GRPO with a pytest reward on Kaggle | 1-2 h smoke, then 7-9 GPU-h for 300 steps | Reward curve, repaired share, STUCK | +2 to +3 runs over rung 3/4 | At least a few dozen mixed-outcome scenarios |

**Rung 1 is a diagnostic, not a fix.** Setting `lora_alpha = 16·s` in a copy of `adapter_config.json` before the existing merge and export scales the delta, and `peft.helpers.rescale_adapter_scale` does the same and is equivalent to WiSE-FT ([PEFT helpers](https://huggingface.co/docs/peft/package_reference/helpers)). Alternatively, serve the base with `--lora-scaled` on a llama.cpp build that includes the 2026-09-30 Qwen3.5 LoRA-conversion fix ([llama.cpp #28324](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/28324)). Scaling LoRA's top intruder dimension to 0.3-0.5 **cut forgetting by 25-33% at almost no accuracy cost** on LLaMA2-7B ([Illusion of Equivalence](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21228v3)), but weight averaging on Qwen3-4B gave back most of the domain gain (0.578 to 0.382) while recovering general ability ([SFT Doesn't Always Hurt](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20758)). Since v2 is not better than base on any metric, the sweep should land near base, not above it; its value is confirming that the delta is net-harmful rather than a useful direction buried in noise.

**Rung 2 decides whether self-training can beat the base at all**, because every self-training recipe only sharpens pass@1 toward pass@k. It has two parts. The headroom part samples k=8 independent first attempts at temperature 0.7 from the untuned Q4_K_M model: 840 samples on 105 scenarios, about 1.5 hours with Ollama on the GPU pipelined against Docker on the CPU at roughly **600 verified samples an hour** ([feasibility note](../research_notes/AeroPatch%20model%20improvement/feasibility_t4_laptop.md)). The loop part runs the real three-attempt loop about three times per scenario, which the student pass did for 189 scenarios in 54 minutes ([NOTES.md](../NOTES.md)), so that rungs 3 and 4 get on-policy repair turns and attempt-2/3 states. Some of this already exists: the student-pass run file holds **156 of the 4B's own resolved edits (111 at attempt 1, 29 at attempt 2, 16 at attempt 3) and 35 REGRESSION attempts** with full proposals (`runs/20261009-student-pass.jsonl`, checked for this report). Those cover all 189 scenarios and were sampled with thinking off, so they need the leakage filter and must match the served mode. The pool is 105 scenarios rather than 189 because the leakage step removed 39 of the 144 synthetic scenarios that reached it ([stats-v2](../training/stats-v2.md)), and every new target must pass the 13-token leakage check again, since the 4B's text differs from the reference fix. Up to 25 more, dropped only because their reference fix used names the prompt hides, can return if the 4B's own fix clears the same checks. Even so, the pool is **an order of magnitude smaller than the 1,000+ problems behind the ReST-EM and RFT gains**, which caps the lift. Scenarios solved in some but not all samples are the most useful training data ([Gekhman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05904)), and their count also sets how much gradient GRPO would get later.

**Rung 3 removes the cause.** It trains only on the 4B's own sandbox-passing outputs, first attempts plus on-policy repair turns from the loop rollouts, capped at one or two per scenario and favouring partly-solved scenarios. It leaves out never-solved scenarios, the 275 raw commit diffs and the 53 offline repair turns. It retrains from base, not from v2, at **lr 2e-5 to 5e-5 for one epoch** with r=16 and alpha=16 unchanged, so only the data and the learning rate move. One check confirms the data is on-policy before training: the targets' mean NLL under the base should fall far below v2's, a few minutes in the Kaggle session. The evidence is real but bounded. ReST-EM gained **5.6-6.4 points on APPS** over the base and regressed after a second iteration ([ReST-EM](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.06585)), and RFT lifted LLaMA-7B on GSM8K from 35.9% to 49.3% ([RFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01825)). Yet self-SFT at lr 1e-4 forgot as much as off-policy SFT on two of three Llama-1B targets ([Retaining by Doing](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18874v3)), SFT on Qwen3-32B's own samples degraded instruction following ([Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)), and SWE-Gym's self-improvement round **fell from 15.3% to 8.7%** ([SWE-Gym](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.21139)). Hence the low learning rate, the single epoch and a cap of two iterations. The expected result, a judgement from these effect sizes, is **parity: test 36-41 of 54 with STUCK back near 0-2**. The Tier B figure of about 6-8 of 20 is weaker. The pool is synthetic-only and drops every real-advisory target, and whether self-training on synthetic CWE scenarios transfers to real CVEs is untested. Because the project's rule also requires no loss on Tier B, that transfer is the rung's main risk.

**Rung 4 targets REGRESSION** with the rollouts rung 2 already produced. Chosen edits pass the PoC and regression tests, and rejected edits end in REGRESSION or POC_FAIL. Keep-behaviour pairs prefer unchanged correct code over an over-hardened edit, the idea of ProSec's D_norm, which **raised utility from 43.4 to 46.1** ([ProSec](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12882)). For STUCK, pairs built at attempts 2-3 and conditioned on the test feedback can teach a change of course. Chosen is the model's own changed, passing retry; rejected is its own failed edit resubmitted unchanged. The base rarely repeats itself (the student pass ended only 4 of 189 scenarios in STUCK), so that rejected side is constructed, though it is still the model's own text. Nothing in the literature tests this pairing. ProSec's objective comparison favours IPO and SimPO, while ORPO **pushed utility below the original model** (40.65 against 42.78) ([ProSec](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12882)). An independent reproduction found ProSec's offline pairs cut Qwen2.5-Coder-7B's HumanEval+ by about 20 points ([SecCoderX](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07422v1)). Execution-verified, self-sampled pairs are a stronger signal than ProSec's analyser labels, but no published result tests the combination. On the T4, set `bf16=False` explicitly, keep each side of a pair to 2,048 tokens since a 2×4,096 pair needs about 19 GB, and drop over-length pairs rather than truncating them, because TRL keeps only the start, which is the part both answers share ([TRL DPO docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/main/en/dpo_trainer)).

**Rung 5 is the only family with clean evidence of teaching the use of feedback**, and also the costliest and riskiest. Multi-turn RL took Llama 3.1 8B **from 10.5 to 16.0 where multi-turn SFT gave 10.0** ([RLEF](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02089)). Murphy's multi-turn GRPO added 4.38 and 4.20 points on Qwen3-4B on LiveCodeBench medium and hard, on 8xH100 for about 11 hours, about 88 H100-hours by the research note's arithmetic ([Murphy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07833)). Online RL kept utility on Qwen2.5-Coder-7B (**HumanEval+ 79.88 to 82.74**) where the offline methods lost about 18-20 points ([SecCoderX](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07422v1)). The reward should demand both suites for full credit, as DeepSWE requires its PASS_TO_PASS and FAIL_TO_PASS tests to pass ([DeepSWE](https://www.together.ai/blog/deepswe)). It should give partial credit by fraction of tests passed, with regression weighted at least as heavily as the PoC: ratio rewards beat binary ones **0.22 to 0.042 at 90 tasks**, though that paper labels its metric ambiguously ([Practitioner's Guide](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01132)). It should give 0 for a gate rejection or an identical resubmission, and add SCoRe's improvement bonus α(r₂ − r₁) on later attempts ([SCoRe](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12917)). The hack to guard against is deleting the vulnerable code path so the PoC passes because the feature is gone, so each scenario's regression tests must be audited to exercise the patched function before any RL. Groups where every sample passes or every sample fails carry no gradient ([DAPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14476)), which makes the rung-2 count of mixed-outcome scenarios the go/no-go.

The engineering risk is high. Text-only GRPO on Qwen3.5 has open shape-mismatch bugs ([unsloth#5121](https://github.com/unslothai/unsloth/issues/5121)). The only Qwen3.5-4B GRPO reference ran at **about 80 s a step on a T4** in fp32 fallback without vLLM ([Unsloth notebook](https://github.com/unslothai/notebooks/blob/main/nb/Qwen3_5_(4B)_Vision_GRPO.ipynb)). The feasibility note extrapolates that to 7-9 hours for the 300-step minimum and 22-31 hours, the whole weekly quota, for 1,000 steps. Kaggle has no Docker, so rewards need a stdlib subprocess runner that mirrors `docker/run.sh`: rlimits through an exec wrapper, a process-group kill, a stripped environment and internet off. It must be calibrated against the laptop's Docker verdicts under Python 3.13 before training. That is feasible because all 189 synthetic lockfiles are stdlib-only ([feasibility note](../research_notes/AeroPatch%20model%20improvement/feasibility_t4_laptop.md)). The laptop cannot share the GRPO loop, because weights change every 1.5 minutes while a GGUF round trip takes hours.

Two options are ruled out by the constraints. On-policy distillation from qwen3.5:9b needs teacher logits on the student's samples, a 16-bit 9B does not fit on a T4 beside the student, and whether Ollama returns usable logprobs is unverified. The 2026 self-distillation method **lags plain SFT at 3B** and costs 2.5x the FLOPs and 4x the wall clock ([SDFT 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19897)), a weak fit at 4B on Kaggle time.

## The four classes the base never fixes need recipes, not commits

**Three of the four dev scenarios the control always fails were never resolved by any v2 model either**: a salted KDF with its verifier (`A-328-01`), SSRF that needs a resolved-IP check (`A-918-01`) and open-redirect variants (`A-601-01`), 0 of 9 v2 dev runs each (the first v2's six D19 runs and the retrain's three; run files `20261013-15*` and `20261019-v2r*-repair-local-dev`). **The fourth is the one place tuning helped**: the two-file SQL injection `A-089-02` was resolved in 3 of those 9 runs (once by the first v2 at step 64, twice by the retrain), and never in the control's 9 dev runs ([NOTES.md](../NOTES.md), D19). Why is not checked; one possible reason is the multi-site changes among the advisory fixes, so that part of the off-policy data carries something useful. A self-training-only rung 3 would drop it, which is worth watching on this scenario. The other three cannot be reached by self-training, because a scenario the model never solves yields no passing sample, and RL gets no gradient from it. Knowledge at inference is the cheaper lever. Removing domain knowledge cost GPT-4.1 **21 of 230 PatchEval fixes** ([PatchEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11019v1)), and retrieving security guidelines with examples added **4.8 SecurePass@1** over five baselines ([RESCUE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18204)). A short, hand-checked recipe per CWE, injected when the finding matches, costs no GPU time. "Resolve the host, then reject private, loopback and link-local addresses with `ipaddress`" is one; "reject `//` and `/\` prefixes" is another. qwen3.5:9b can draft both the recipes and new synthetic scenarios for these classes within the free-data rule, and those scenarios can turn never-solved cases into partly-solved ones that rungs 3-5 can learn from. Once retrieval is part of the served configuration, base and tuned models must be compared with it switched on.

The free-only rule shapes the data as much as the method. ProSec's data was synthesised with a commercial model ([ProSec](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12882)), Vul-R2's reasoning traces came from o3 ([Vul-R2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05480v1)), and Repair-R1's bugs were created by GPT-4o ([Repair-R1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22853)), so only their recipes transfer. The project's sandbox is the asset none of those papers had. Every published small-model security-training method relied on static analysers or proxy rewards, while **AeroPatch has an executable PoC and regression suite for every synthetic scenario**, a stronger and cheaper reward than any of them used ([vulnerability repair note](../research_notes/AeroPatch%20model%20improvement/vulnerability_repair_methods.md)).

## Conclusion

The fine-tune lost because of whose words it was trained on, not because of epochs, rank or the share of repair turns, and that changes what "more training data" should mean for AeroPatch. Another 26-hour generation run of reference fixes would add more off-policy targets. A few hours of sampling the model's own verified fixes produces the kind of data that preserves the base's skill and reveals the headroom before any GPU-hour is spent, and part of that data already sits in the student-pass run file.

The harder truth is statistical. Published gains at 4-8B (+4 to +6 points) are 2-3 runs of 54, inside the benchmark's noise, and untrained configuration changes have already moved the control by +3, each judged "no difference shown". **The binding constraint on showing improvement is evaluation power, not method**, so the cheapest high-value work is a bigger executable dev pool, same-day controls and four runs per model. If the goal is the best served system rather than a fine-tune that beats its base, inference-side sampling, thinking mode and CWE recipes are cheaper than any training rung, and training should then be judged against that raised bar, not against 38.

## Corrections after checking against the run files (2026-10-21)

- **`A-089-02` is not evidence that the v2 data helped.** The library `qwen3.5:4b` build, the same base weights, resolved it in 3 of 12 `repair-local` dev runs (`20261001-175057`, `20261001-182050`, `20261003-113223`), on the same scenario image (`sha256:1db9f3db8ea4`) the control later ran. Pooled over every local dev run, the base resolves it 3 of 16 times and the tuned checkpoints 4 of 19. The "0 of 9" above counts only the `aeropatch-control` export's runs.
- **Two dev scenarios, not four, are never fixed by the base**: `A-328-01` (0 of 37 local dev runs, tuned ones included) and `A-918-01` (2 of 37). `A-601-01` was fixed 5 of 18 times by the base and 0 of 19 by the tuned models, so it is a scenario the tuning lost, not one the base cannot reach. The CWE-recipe idea therefore rests on two dev scenarios.
- **The chat template is not an untested confound.** D17 checked that Ollama and the Hugging Face template build the same prompt: token counts are equal on 25 sampled prompts, and `finetune.py` repeats the check on every example that carries Ollama's count.
- **Rung 3 as specified barely trains.** About 100-180 own-fix examples for one epoch at batch 8 is 13-23 optimizer steps at 2e-5, against v2's 64 steps at 2e-4, a weight change roughly 30-50 times smaller. Landing at parity would then be the expected result of almost not training, not evidence about on-policy data. A run that can test the diagnosis changes one variable: v2's learning rate and step count, with the model's own verified fixes in place of v2's targets, weighted toward attempt-2/3 fixes and scenarios solved only in some samples.
- **The training pool is close to saturated for the base.** On the 105 leakage-clean synthetic scenarios, the student pass resolved 74 at attempt 1 and 93 within three attempts. Self-training there sharpens what the base mostly does already; the gap to close is on Tier A test and Tier B, which the pool does not represent.

## What rungs 2 and 3 measured (2026-10-21)

- **Rung 2, headroom**: the untuned model, one attempt at temperature 0.7, 8 times on the 105 scenarios: **pass@1 70.4%, pass@8 89.5%**, 49 scenarios partly solved, 11 never. Both go-on thresholds passed, and the ceiling is close: the base reaches about 90% of this pool within 8 tries.
- **Rung 3, as corrected above** (v2's learning rate, LoRA settings and 64 steps, on 144 of the model's own sandbox-passing answers, 128 first attempts and 16 repair turns; the data's mix, size and epochs changed with it, and the linear decay ending at step 64 made the total update about two-thirds of v2's). The first training loss was 0.158 against v2's 0.385, so the data is on-policy. Against the untuned control alternated with it on the same day: **test 38 against 41 of 54** (p = 0.58), **Tier B 10 against 8 of 20**, Tier C 8 against 6 of 10. No difference shown on any tier, the parity this report predicted. `STUCK` endings on test fell to 4, against v2's 9 and 11; attempts ending in `REGRESSION` are still higher than the control's (30 against 22). This supports the off-policy diagnosis without isolating it, because the mix (synthetic only), size (133 against 511), epochs (about 3.8 against 1) and total update changed together. The untuned model stays the served one.
- **The same-day control moved more than any training did**: the untuned model scored 16, 16, 20 and 21 of 27 on test today, against 19 and 19 on D20. Evaluation power, not method, remains the binding constraint.
- For rung 4, the 840 samples give only 19 scenarios with both a passing and a `REGRESSION` edit, short of the roughly 100 pairs the rung's go-on condition asks for.
